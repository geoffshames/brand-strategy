01 / DIRECTIONDiscovery → trust → viewing

A small film.
A personal invitation.
A reason to watch.

Papá Melissa already has what an organic campaign needs: the texture of a real family, the patience of a long filmmaker relationship, and a story that can travel through humor, art and love. The task is to help people encounter that world, then give their curiosity somewhere to go.

The September conversation with MZ points toward smaller film and culture voices, early viewing and approved unseen material. The release brief gives that approach a clear destination: New York attendance first, followed by conversation that carries into the digital window.

Start with a small creator circle and the editorial communities already close to the film. Offer access the team can deliver, invite a considered response in each person’s own voice, and connect every piece to the correct ticket or viewing destination. A three-to-five-creator pilot is a recommended scope, not a promise of participation.

Planning basis: September 21 strategy call and September 22 “Find the Film” brief. Reconfirm release details, guest access and available seats before sending invitations.
THE FAMILY FIRST

Lead with life.

A funny, tender or creative moment opens the door. Melissa, Claudia and their family remain people with their own story and language.

A DECADE OF TRUST

Offer a closer look.

A filmmaker conversation and an approved scene give a thoughtful creator substance to work with. The access must be specific and available.

THE LATE DISCOVERY

Give curiosity a destination.

Let independent film voices explain why this discovery matters. Then move audiences toward the New York run and the confirmed digital window.

The strategic read +

Strength

A distinctive family story with documented festival support and existing coverage creates credible reasons to engage.

Constraint

A short runway and limited cash require a small, personal invitation list. Guest access and approved materials determine what can be offered.

Opportunity

Previous coverage and Latin American film communities offer warmer entry points than a broad cold creator campaign.

Risk

Identity-led curiosity or unclear access promises could distort the film’s framing. The team should approve invitations and clip context before use.

03 / IDEASSix proposed activations

Make access useful.

Each invitation needs a clear offer, a manageable ask and a next step for the audience. Use the first two ideas for the New York pilot, then extend the best responses toward digital.

01PILOT / FIRST LOOK

The First-Look Circle

Bring a few thoughtful film and culture voices into the story before the New York run. A small conversation gives each person a perspective worth sharing.

Offer
Private screener, approved asset pack, and a small filmmaker Q&A if available.
Invite
One original video, essay or newsletter item with screening information. Agree on scope only with participating collaborators.
Measure
Accepted invitations, published pieces and visits to the ticket destination.
Best with: Juan Ayala, Rosa Parra, Latinx Lens
02PILOT / SCENE RESPONSE

One Scene That Stayed

A precise observation is more compelling than a general endorsement. Let a creator explain why one approved moment stayed with them.

Offer
Screener plus a small choice of permitted excerpts and stills.
Invite
A short scene response in the creator’s own voice. Keep spoilers within the agreed boundaries.
Measure
Saves, substantive comments and screening-link clicks.
Best with: film critics and scene-analysis creators
03CULTURE / PERSONAL STORY

The Things Our Families Teach Us

A family ritual, creative practice or lesson can connect the film to a creator’s own experience without asking them to explain Melissa’s identity.

Offer
Early viewing and a conversation about an approved theme.
Invite
One personal story ending with an invitation to watch. Family participation and personal disclosure are optional.
Measure
Thoughtful responses, shares and visits to the viewing destination.
Best with: creative-life and family storytellers
04ART / SMALL COLLABORATION

An Artist’s Response

Invite an illustrator, photographer or visual storyteller to respond to the film’s creative world with a small work and an accompanying reflection.

Offer
Approved imagery and access to a filmmaker discussion.
Invite
A modest visual response with attribution and a viewing link. Agree on reuse rights first.
Measure
Saves, shares and attributable visits.
Larger commissions require a separate agreed fee
05NEW YORK / IN THE ROOM

What We Carried Home

Turn opening week into an honest account of being in the room: the marquee, the conversation and what people took away from the film.

Offer
Available screening seats and a short hosted discussion, subject to venue and guest confirmation.
Invite
A recap using two prompts: “What felt familiar?” and “What stayed with you?” Film only people who opt in.
Measure
Participation, usable responses and subsequent ticket interest.
A moderated response wall can extend the conversation

OPTIONAL CASH CEILING
$0

WHAT TO RECORD

Follow the viewing path.

Keep one invitation tracker and a unique link per participating creator. Record the offered access, agreed scope, publication and destination. Compare useful interest, not follower counts alone.

  1. Invited → acceptedTrack whether the access offer creates interest.
  2. Published → clickedRecord pieces, thoughtful responses and unique-link visits.
  3. Clicked → attended or viewedUse ticket or platform reporting where available. Clicks alone are not attendance or views.
06 / NEXT MOVESReady for team review

Give the invitation
something to promise.

  1. Lock the access menu. Confirm the screener process, approved clips, seats, Q&A format and who can actually attend.
  2. Select the first eight invitations. Start with existing coverage, one NYC host and film/community voices with a clear reason to care.
  3. Agree a compact pilot. Seek three to five participating creators. One original piece each is a proposed collaboration scope; independent critics decide their own coverage.
  4. Prepare the destination. Reconfirm the New York ticket link and the digital release date, eligible territories and email flow before traffic arrives.
  5. Approve the framing. The film team handles sensitive identity, family and minor-related questions. Clear clips and audience capture before use; respect independent reviews.
A sample creator invitation +

We’re helping bring Papá Melissa to New York for its Cinema Village run, October 23–29. It’s a documentary about Melissa, Claudia and their family in Coyoacán, told through the everyday texture of their lives.

Your work around [specific relevant piece] made us think you might connect with it. We’d love to offer an early look and, if schedules allow, a small conversation with the filmmaking team. If it resonates, would you be open to discussing one piece in your own voice that invites people to see it?

We can confirm the available access, approved material and timing before you decide. No expectation of a positive review.

Customize before sending. Dates and access must be reconfirmed. This is a draft; no outreach has been sent.
